# Motion

The Showcase story holds replayable motion swatches with a reduced-motion switch, the live rulebook and the accessibility coverage. This guide holds the reasoning.

## 1. Introduction

**In plain words:** Motion is how things move between states. Small, quick movement tells users something changed. Long or looping movement gets in the way.

**Precisely:** Foundation. Three durations, capped at 400ms. It governs transitions and the spinner.

**Three steps, three roles.** Tier 1 holds the three durations. Tier 2 names the jobs: fast (hover, press), base (small enter and exit), deliberate (the ceiling, one spinner turn). Three easing curves sit beside the durations: `motion.ease.standard` (stays on screen), `motion.ease.enter` (arrives) and `motion.ease.exit` (leaves). The spinner uses `linear`, because a turn must not speed up and slow down.

## 2. Usage

### When to use

- For feedback on hover and press, and for a spinner that shows work is running (Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status).

### When not to use, and what to use instead

- **To decorate.** Skip it. Motion with no job is noise.
- **For content that moves on its own for more than 5 seconds.** Give a way to pause (WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide, A).

### How

- **Tokens.** Read `motion.duration.*` in every `transition` and `animation`.
- **Preference.** Stop looping animation under `prefers-reduced-motion` (WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions, AAA; house standard at AA).

## 3. Why the states look as they do

A foundation has no interaction state. Its one condition is the user's motion preference. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, looping animation stops and transitions shorten to none. The Showcase simulates this with a switch, so you can compare the two behaviours side by side without changing the operating system setting.

## 4. Pitfalls and don'ts

- **Do not run a 600ms transition.** Users wait for the interface. (House cap, `house.maxDurationMs`; `motion.duration-ceiling`)
- **Do not ship a spinner that ignores reduced motion.** Vestibular disorders make motion harmful. (WCAG 2.3.3, AAA; `motion.reduced-motion`)
