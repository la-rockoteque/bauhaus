# Motion

## 1. Introduction

**In plain words:** Motion is how things move between states. Small, quick movement tells users something changed. Long or looping movement gets in the way.

**Precisely:** Foundation · three durations, capped at 400ms · governs transitions and the spinner.

## 2. Tokens (defined)

| Token | Tier | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `duration.150`, `200`, `400` | 1 | 150, 200, 400 ms |
| `motion.duration.fast` | 2 | `{duration.150}`; hover and press |
| `motion.duration.base` | 2 | `{duration.200}`; small enter and exit |
| `motion.duration.deliberate` | 2 | `{duration.400}`; the ceiling; one spinner turn |

## 3. Anatomy

Three steps on a tier 1 scale, three roles on tier 2. Easing is not tokenised in this template; the spinner uses `linear`.

<Canvas of={Stories.Durations} />

## 4. States

n/a for a foundation. Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, looping animation stops and transitions shorten to none.

## 5. Usage

### When to use

- For feedback on hover and press, and for a spinner that shows work is running (Nielsen heuristic 1, visibility of system status).

### When not to use — and what instead

- To decorate: skip it. Motion with no job is noise.
- For content that moves on its own for more than 5 seconds: give a way to pause (WCAG 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide, A).

### How

- Read `motion.duration.*` in every `transition` and `animation`.
- Stop looping animation under `prefers-reduced-motion` (WCAG 2.3.3, AAA; house standard at AA).

## 6. Pitfalls and don'ts

| Don't | Why it fails | Basis | Rule id |
|---|---|---|---|
| A 600ms transition | Users wait for the interface | House cap, `house.maxDurationMs` | `motion.duration-ceiling` |
| A spinner that ignores reduced motion | Vestibular disorders make motion harmful | WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) | `motion.reduced-motion` |
